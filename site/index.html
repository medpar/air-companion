Talk to your band. Directly.

Pebble is a native, offline iPhone app for the Fitbit Air. It speaks the band's own Bluetooth protocol in short authenticated sessions: alarms, vibration alerts, live heart rate and honest training trends. No account, no cloud, and Google Health keeps syncing.

01 · How it works

Six layers between your phone and your wrist.

The Air uses Google's open-source Golden Gate stack: a tiny IP network carried over Bluetooth LE, secured with DTLS, speaking CoAP with protobuf payloads. Pebble embeds the same stack in a small C bridge that exposes only a fixed list of routes. Pick a layer.

↑ iPhone · PebbleFitbit Air ↓

      One request, wrapped down to this layer

        The handshake

        The band is the client. The phone is the server.

        Every Air has two 16-byte pre-shared keys, primary and secondary, each with a 32-bit ID. The band opens with a PSK identity naming the key; Pebble looks it up in the Keychain and answers as the DTLS server.

        Where the keys come from

        Your band's keys, read once.

        The keys live in Google's cloud. For this project they were recovered with a scoped, single-use mitmproxy addon that watched the official app's own traffic and made a bounded, single-use read of the paired Air's keys. It never stores credentials, cookies or unrelated bodies.

        Before any custom byte went to the band, a live Bluetooth trace was decrypted offline: both Finished messages verified and all 131 application records parsed as CoAP.

        Pebble imports the key file once, moves it into the device-only Keychain and deletes the file. Read the method →

        One owner at a time

        Borrow the band, then give it back.

        The Air accepts one secure session at a time. Holding it would stop Google Health syncing, so every Pebble job is connect → handshake → one task → disconnect, with a 35 s hard limit. Background step checks come sooner only when your pace is about to cross a goal, and never more than every 5 minutes.

        02 · Signals

        What the band says, and what it can be made to do.

        Reads are snapshots you ask for. Live heart rate streams over a standard profile. Events arrive when the band chooses to send them. Vibration has no command of its own, only a side effect.

        Reads

        Snapshots on request

        Steps and heart rate from /liveactivity, battery from /device/info, alarms and settings. Each reading is timestamped, so an old count is never shown as today's.

        Activity-only read: about 3 s. Full sync with alarms, capabilities, haptics and battery: about 5 s.

        Live heart rate

        Standard Heart Rate Profile

        With Share heart rate on in Google Health, the Air also exposes Bluetooth service 0x180D, measurement 0x2A37: bpm plus RR intervals in 1/1024 s.

        Pebble turns it into heart-rate-reserve zones, RMSSD over the last 120 beats and Edwards training load. No keys needed for this path.

        Events · band → phone

        Four letters, one integer

        The band is a CoAP client too. It POSTs an EventSet to /events and /eventsSecure on the phone. IDs are four ASCII characters packed big-endian.

        This Air has only ever sent rset. Pebble's Listen for band events screen shows any code that arrives, unknown ones included.

        Event decoder

        Try the encoding

        The seven IDs defined by the shared Fitbit protocol. Wrist on and off (wrs+, wrs-) are the only interaction events, and this Air hasn't emitted them. A dot marks what was actually observed.

        Vibration

        There's no buzz command. So Pebble flips a setting.

        Changing the haptic intensity from Low to High makes the band play a preview. Pebble toggles it, holds for 0.3 s and restores it. Every change is a Bluetooth round trip of about a second, so patterns differ by count and spacing, not rhythm. Gaps under about 1.2 s feel identical on the wrist.

        What it can't do

        Four things the Air won't do from an iPhone.

        Each was checked against a Bluetooth trace or static analysis of the official app, then left alone rather than guessed at.

        Other apps' notifications

        Bands that mirror iPhone notifications use Apple's ANCS from firmware. The Air never subscribed in a trace, and iOS gives apps no access. Shortcuts automations → Buzz Band fill the gap.

        LED colour

        No app-addressable LED exists for any Fitbit model. The Air's light is firmware battery status: white at 20 % and above, red below.

        Tap triggers

        Double-tap only dismisses alarms on the band. No tap or gesture event is exported, and there's no accelerometer stream.

        Firmware haptic patterns

        A notification record with a hapticPattern field exists, but it's never been seen on the Air's wire and its values are undocumented. Pebble never sends it.

        03 · The Pebble app

        Small, quiet and honest about its numbers.

        Four tabs: Today, Alarms, Trends and Band. Every figure says where it came from and when, and every method is explained in the app with its citation.

        Today and Alarms

        Pull to sync. Steps, heart rate and battery come straight from the band; Readiness combines your own HRV, resting heart rate, sleep and regularity and shows each part.

        • Add, edit, repeat and delete alarms — with smart wake, verified by reading back
        • Readiness 0–100 — HRV 40, resting HR 25, sleep 25, regularity 10
        • Shortcuts & Siri — Buzz Band, Get Steps, Get Band Battery

        Trends and Band

        Trends compares your last week with your own previous 60 days, never with a population average. Band is where alerts live: each event gets its own pattern, plus an optional iPhone notification or webhook.

        • Incoming calls — via CXCallObserver, Urgent pattern by default
        • Daily goal, step milestones, move reminder — journaled first, never replayed
        • Keep Pebble running — opt-in silent audio session for alerts while locked
        • Webhooks — {"event":"goal","steps":10240,…} for Home Assistant or n8n
        Methods

        Every number has a citation.

        Missing days are never zero, Apple Health sources are never blended, and nothing is a diagnosis. Full detail in METHODS.md.

        Usual range

        last 7 d vs previous 60 d

        HRV log-scaled ±0.5 SD (Plews 2013); resting HR and sleep ±1 SD.

        Training load

        RPE × minutes

        Session RPE (Foster 2001); fitness and fatigue as 42- and 7-day weighted loads (Banister 1975).

        Workload ratio

        EWMA acute : chronic

        Williams 2017, shown with the 0.8–1.3 band as context, not a rule.

        Cardio fitness

        VO₂max ≈ 15.3 × HRmax / HRrest

        Uth 2004; HRmax = 208 − 0.7 × age (Tanaka 2001); ranked by Cooper Institute norms.

        Sleep timing

        midpoint · 14-night SD

        Regularity of the main episode; social jetlag as weekend vs weekday gap (Wittmann 2006).

        Steps

        ~100 steps/min

        Moderate walking cadence (Tudor-Locke 2018); goal guidance from Paluch 2022.

        04 · Safety model

        Every write is a round trip.

        The C bridge exposes the routes above and nothing else: no firmware, reset, pairing, sync-acknowledgement or arbitrary-command paths.

        1. Read fresh

          Current alarms or settings are read in the same session, never from a cache.

        2. Back up

          The prior state is journaled in protected storage, so an interrupted change recovers on the next connection.

        3. Write once

          One request, no automatic retry. Only the known submessage is sent; unknown fields are kept.

        4. Read back

          The result is compared field by field. A conflicting change is reported, never overwritten.

        5.00

        A firmware quirk, handled on purpose. Creating an alarm returns CoAP 5.00 and enables it even when it was sent disabled. Pebble never repeats the POST: it reads back, finds exactly one new alarm, checks every other alarm is unchanged and applies one corrective PUT.

        05 · Research trail

        From an encrypted trace to a buzz.

        Evidence was graded at every step: live-verified, primary reference, recovered static output or unverified. Nothing was enabled on a guess.

        1. Oct 13

          First Bluetooth trace

          A 90 s PacketLogger capture while the official app opened alarms: 185 IPv4/UDP datagrams reassembled from Gattlink, every one a DTLS 1.2 application record on port 5684.

        2. Oct 13

          Schema from static analysis

          Alarm, settings and capability messages recovered from the Android app's code. The key surprise: PUT /settings is a query, POST is the write.

        3. Oct 13

          Keys, read once

          After fixing two gRPC handling defects in mitmproxy, a scoped, single-use probe read the paired Air's two keys: four GETs in total, no writes. Proxy, certificates and trust were removed afterwards.

        4. Oct 13

          Handshake decrypted offline

          A fresh trace caught the full handshake. Rebuilding the transcript verified both Finished messages; all 131 records parsed as CoAP. The alarm list decoded: capacity 8, smart wake supported.

        5. Oct 13

          First custom connection

          Golden Gate and Mbed TLS embedded in a C bridge. Connected, authenticated and read two alarms in six seconds. Google Health kept syncing.

        6. Oct 15

          Writes on the real band

          Alarm create, edit and delete with exact restoration; vibration by intensity toggle; a reordering bug in Gattlink sends fixed with a bounded FIFO.

        7. Oct 15

          Pebble 1.1

          Countable vibration patterns, a backgrounded CallKit call buzzing the band end to end, and cited health models.

        06 · Build it

        Bring your own band.

        macOS, Xcode with an iOS 18+ SDK, CMake 3.25+. Dependencies are fetched at pinned, hash-checked versions. The offline checks need no phone, band or key.

        # fetch Golden Gate + Mbed TLS, then build for the simulator
$ ./scripts/prepare-native.sh
$ ./scripts/build-simulator.sh
$ xcrun simctl launch booted com.medpar.air.companion --preview

# encrypted synthetic-peer transport, parsers, fitness and storage tests
$ ./scripts/check-offline.sh

# your own phone (free Personal Team signing lasts 7 days)
$ AIR_DEVELOPMENT_TEAM=YOUR_TEAM_ID ./scripts/build-ios.sh
